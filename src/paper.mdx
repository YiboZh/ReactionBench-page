## TL;DR

Video benchmarks ask what happens *in* a video. Socially responsive systems also need to relate what a person **watches** to how that person **responds**: a laugh follows a visual gag; a pause matters only against the same viewer's surrounding behavior. ReactionBench asks whether current multimodal models can rank, temporally match, and explain a viewer's reaction against the stimulus it was watching. Across seven models, the answer is: not yet.

Each task states its input, its supervision source, and its scoring rule. No model-generated annotation is used as gold.

## Building the benchmark

Source videos are public reaction recordings in which a creator watches content, usually in a picture-in-picture layout. The pipeline separates the stimulus and reactor views into time-aligned streams. A model-assisted pre-screen nominates candidate moments, and people supply every label: intensity ratings, rationale references, and the audits behind each task's construction contract.

## Key findings

<div class="not-prose my-8 grid gap-4 md:grid-cols-2">
  <Finding number="50.9%" title="Matching stays hard" color="--rb-t2">
    The best model picks the aligned stimulus in about half of four-way SRM items (chance 25%). Rationale composites peak at 2.91 / 5.
  </Finding>
  <Finding number="≈0" title="Which reaction? Models barely notice" color="--rb-t1">
    For GPT-5, removing the reaction lowers rationale scores, but substituting <em>another viewer's</em> reaction produces no detectable drop. The model responds to the presence of a reaction, not to which reaction it is shown.
  </Finding>
  <Finding number="+.14–.23" title="Protocol moves the score" color="--rb-data">
    Scoring moments independently instead of jointly raises intensity-ranking ρ₀ for every one of the seven models, and every paired interval excludes zero.
  </Finding>
  <Finding number="7 / 7" title="No model leads every column" color="--rb-t3">
    Gemini leads joint IP, GPT-5 leads SRM and cue coverage, Qwen3.6-27B leads independent IP, and Kimi-K2.6 leads the GRG composite. The 27B model beats the 397B mixture on IP and SRM.
  </Finding>
</div>

## Data release and responsible use

- **Code** (MIT): evaluation harness for all three tasks, scoring, judge, and the dataset-construction pipeline.
- **Annotations** (CC BY-NC 4.0): frozen task files, de-identified human ratings, and human rationale references. Annotator identities are replaced with pseudonyms.
- **Evaluation clips** (2,110 clips, about 3 GB) are on a [gated Hugging Face dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/YiboZhaoMit/ReactionBench). Access is reviewed by the authors and granted for non-commercial academic research. Reaction recordings belong to their creators and stimulus content to its rights holders.
- **Creator opt-out and takedown.** Creators can request removal of their videos by emailing [yibozhao@mit.edu](mailto:yibozhao@mit.edu) and [qua@mit.edu](mailto:qua@mit.edu) (see [TAKEDOWN.md](https://github.com/MIT-MI/ReactionBench/blob/main/TAKEDOWN.md)).

ReactionBench is intended for evaluating research systems that connect observable events with observable reactions under a disclosed protocol. It is **not** intended for inferring private mental states, diagnosing emotion or health, detecting deception, ranking people by emotional expressiveness, or supporting surveillance or consequential decisions about individuals. Human-participant research completed institutional ethics review. Annotators were paid an average of \$12.00–\$16.67 per hour.
